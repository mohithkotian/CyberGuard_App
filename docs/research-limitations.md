## 1. Context & Research Scope

Academic and industrial rigor requires transparent disclosure of the empirical boundaries of the CyberGuard platform. This document delineates verified constraints across data, machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, legal workflows, and deployment architecture, connecting each limitation to its analytical implication and outlining concrete future research avenues.

## 2. Evidence-Based Study Limitations

### 2.1 Dataset Scale, Provenance & Synthetic Nature
- **Identified Constraint**: The experimental benchmark operates on $N = 257$ curated text samples across 13 cybercrime attack categories (`ml-service/data/dataset.csv`).
- **Implication**: While representative of typical citizen incident descriptions, the sample size is limited. Deep neural models (such as transformer-based architectures) cannot be reliably fine-tuned on a corpus of this scale without severe overfitting, which justified the selection of TF-IDF and regularized Logistic Regression.
- **Generalizability Risk**: Extreme edge-case incident descriptions or novel multimodal attack vectors not represented in the 257 samples may be misclassified or default to the `other` category.

### 2.2 Class Imbalance & Evaluation Variance
- **Identified Constraint**: Class distribution ranges from 38 samples (`phishing`) down to 9 samples (`other`), reflecting a 4.22:1 imbalance ratio.
- **Implication**: In the 5-fold cross-validation evaluation, individual folds exhibit performance variance (Fold Accuracy ranges from 60.78% in Fold 5 to 78.85% in Fold 2; standard deviation $\pm 0.0802$). Classes with low support (`other`, `crypto_fraud`, `women_child_safety`) show lower recall in certain folds due to the limited number of training instances available to parameterize the L-BFGS decision boundary.

### 2.3 Absence of Probability Calibration in Production
- **Identified Constraint**: The classifier applies standard Multinomial Logistic Regression ($C=4.0$, balanced class weights). Probability calibration (such as Platt scaling via `CalibratedClassifierCV`) is not fitted in the production pipeline.
- **Implication**: The confidence score returned by `/predict` represents a raw softmax transformation of log-odds ($\sigma(z)_i$). These scores do not strictly conform to empirical posterior probabilities (e.g., a predicted confidence of 0.85 does not mathematically guarantee that 85 out of 100 cases are truly positive). Researchers and law enforcement operators must interpret confidence as relative decision margin rather than statistical certainty.

### 2.4 Monolingual English NLP Scope
- **Identified Constraint**: The lexical preprocessor and stop-word dictionaries are tailored exclusively to English.
- **Implication**: In multicultural and linguistically diverse jurisdictions (such as India, where complaints frequently feature regional vernacular or code-mixed Hinglish/Kannada/Tamil transliteration), raw non-English text may yield uninformative sparse feature vectors, necessitating reliance on the keyword heuristic fallback.

### 2.5 OCR Optical Sensitivity & Layout Variations
- **Identified Constraint**: OCR utilizes Tesseract.js operating on English OCR models. While financial regex post-processors reliably extract 12-digit UTR references and Rupee amounts from clean payment screenshots, degraded image inputs (e.g., low-resolution mobile photographs of computer monitors, heavily compressed WhatsApp images, or non-standard UPI banking skins) experience character segmentation errors.
- **Implication**: OCR-derived data cannot be assumed 100% forensically conclusive without officer visual cross-verification against raw uploaded evidence.

### 2.6 Deterministic Pattern-Based PII Masking
- **Identified Constraint**: `privacyShield.js` operates on 18 deterministic regular expression stages rather than an Named Entity Recognition (NER) token classification model.
- **Implication**: Highly structured entities (Aadhaar, PAN, emails, URLs, phone numbers, IPv4/IPv6) achieve near-perfect detection precision; however, unstructured entities (such as contextual victim person names or informal physical addresses without street keywords) present higher false-negative rates unless accompanied by labeling keywords.

### 2.7 Statutory Notice Automation Boundaries
- **Identified Constraint**: Statutory outputs generated by the platform (Section 65B Electronic Evidence Certificates, Section 94 BNSS Bank Freezes, Section 173 BNSS Zero-FIR transfer dockets, and Telecom Requisition notices) are **procedural drafts**.
- **Implication**: CyberGuard does not possess direct programmatic API access to core banking networks (NPCI / SFMS) or telecom law enforcement portals. All notices require human review, institutional authorization, and formal digital signature by a designated nodal officer.

### 2.8 Single-Node Deployment & In-Memory Rate Limiting
- **Identified Constraint**: The Flask ML microservice utilizes in-memory storage for `Flask-Limiter` (`storage_uri='memory://'`).
- **Implication**: In single-worker container environments (e.g., Render `-w 1`), rate limits are accurately enforced. However, horizontal scaling across multiple container replicas would require an external state store (such as Redis) to synchronize rate counters globally.

## 3. Prioritized Future Research & Engineering Roadmap

The following enhancements are proposed as future research directions:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                              PRIORITIZED FUTURE WORK                                    |
|                                                                                         |
|  [HIGH PRIORITY]                                                                        |
|    1. Multilingual & Code-Mixed NLP Support (IndicBERT / MuRIL / Multilingual DistilBERT)  |
|    2. Population-Scale Empirical Corpus (Collate 5,000+ anonymized real-world cases)    |
|    3. Probability Calibration Integration (Platt Scaling & Isotonic Calibration via CV)|
|                                                                                         |
|  [MEDIUM PRIORITY]                                                                      |
|    4. Transformer-Based Named Entity Recognition (Token-level contextual PII masking)   |
|    5. Computer Vision Receipt Forgery Model (Error Level Analysis & CNN artifact detect)|
|    6. Centralized Distributed Rate Limiting & Audit Ledger (Redis + Hash-Chained DB)   |
|                                                                                         |
|  [STATUTORY / INTEGRATION PRIORITY]                                                     |
|    7. National Cybercrime Portal Direct API Interoperability (I4C / 1930 CFCFRMS)       |
|    8. Hardware Security Module (HSM) Cryptographic Key Signing for Section 65B Certs   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

1. **Multilingual NLP for Vernacular Cybercrime**:
   - Integrate pre-trained multilingual language models (e.g., IndicBERT, MuRIL) capable of understanding regional dialectic complaint narratives and phonetic Romanized spellings.
2. **Empirical Population-Scale Validation**:
   - Partner with legal aid clinics and law enforcement cyber cells to curate a benchmark dataset of 5,000+ anonymized real-world incident filings across urban and rural demographics.
3. **Calibrated Confidence Modeling**:
   - Implement out-of-fold Platt scaling (`CalibratedClassifierCV(method='sigmoid', cv='prefit')`) so that confidence percentages mathematically represent true empirical likelihoods.
4. **Contextual Token-Classification NER**:
   - Augment regex masking with transformer-based NER to detect unconventional person names, unformatted rural addresses, and emerging cryptocurrency transaction hashes.
5. **Computer Vision-Based Receipt Tampering Detection**:
   - Supplement OCR text parsing with Error Level Analysis (ELA) and convolutional neural networks to detect spliced pixels, cloned font glyphs, and image manipulation in mobile payment screenshots.
